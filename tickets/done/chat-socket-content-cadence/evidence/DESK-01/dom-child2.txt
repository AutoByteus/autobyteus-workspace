The ebb and flow of tides, those rhythmic breathing rhythms of the ocean, arise from a delicate gravitational ballet played out between the Earth, the Moon, and the Sun. The Moon, our nearest celestial neighbor, holds primary responsibility for creating tides despite being far smaller than the Sun, simply because it sits so much closer to our planet. Gravity, that invisible force binding all objects with mass, pulls more strongly on the side of Earth facing the Moon than on the planet’s center, and even more weakly on the far side. This differential pull stretches the ocean waters, creating two tidal bulges on opposite sides of the globe simultaneously. As Earth rotates on its axis once every twenty-four hours, any given coastal location passes through these bulges, experiencing two high tides and two low tides within each lunar day, which spans roughly twenty-four hours and fifty minutes.

The Sun contributes to tidal forces as well, though its effect amounts to only about forty percent of the Moon’s influence. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align during new and full moon phases, their gravitational pulls reinforce each other, producing especially high high tides and especially low low tides known as spring tides. Conversely, when the Sun and Moon sit at right angles relative to Earth during quarter moon phases, their forces partially cancel each other out, yielding milder neap tides with smaller differences between high and low water levels. Local geography dramatically modifies these baseline tidal patterns. Bays, estuaries, and continental shelves can amplify, delay, or even reverse expected tidal behavior through resonance effects and friction with the ocean floor. Some locations experience only one tide per day, while others exhibit complex mixed patterns. Over geological timescales, tidal forces gradually slow Earth’s rotation through friction between moving water and shallow seafloors, lengthening our days by approximately two milliseconds per century, while simultaneously pushing the Moon slowly farther away from our planet.
